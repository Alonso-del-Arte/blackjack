# Strategy suggestions for blackjack

[The rules of blackjack](GameRules.md) allow moves that are inadvisable. In this 
document, we give some general guidance, some of it based on widespread 
agreement.

Still, since I have limited experience playing in a mock casino and no 
experience playing the game in a real casino for real money, any advice in this 
document should be treated with a grain of salt.

Although there is some allusion to the general principles of card counting, and 
even though card counting is technically not illegal, I do not encourage it. 
Casinos tend to take a very dim view of card counting. Therefore in this 
document I won't be giving any advice on any specific card counting techniques, 
other than that you might want to occasionally lose some bets on purpose.

## Splitting

* **Aces** &mdash; There is near universal agreement you should always split 
Aces. A pair of Aces gets counted as 12 so that you don't bust, but if you hit 
on that hand you run the risk of busting if you get a Ten or a royal card. The 
worst case scenario for splitting Aces is that you get more Aces, but at least 
there's no immediate risk of going bust.

* **Twos** &mdash; I'm not sure there's much point to splitting Twos, but people 
who know better tend to say you should split if the dealer's face up card is a 
low value card, up to maybe a Six or a Seven.

* **Threes** &mdash; FINISH WRITING

* **Fours** &mdash; FINISH WRITING

* **Fives** &mdash; FINISH WRITING

* **Sixes** &mdash; Maybe split if the dealer has a low face up card up to and 
including a Six, otherwise prefer to keep them together.

* **Sevens** &mdash; Experts seems to agree the decision to split Sevens should 
depend on what the dealer's face up card is. If the dealer is showing a low 
card, split your Sevens, but if the dealer is showing a high card, keep your 
Sevens together. There isn't exact agreement on what the threshold is between a 
low card and a high card. Some might consider a dealer's Seven low in this 
context, some might consider it high.

* **Eights** &mdash; There is near universal agreement you should always split 
Eights. If you stand on a pair of Eights, the dealer is likely to beat your 
score (remember that the dealer has to draw above 16 regardless), but hitting on 
a pair of Eights runs a high risk of going bust. Splitting Eights is perhaps 
the best way out of a bad hand.

* **Nines** &mdash; "I never split Nines," says some guy in a TV ad for an 
online casino. Is he supposed to be a famous blackjack player? Even if he is, I 
wouldn't put much stock in any proscription coming from some guy in an online 
casino ad. Splitting Nines might be a good idea _if_ you think the dealer is 
likely to get to 19, 20 or 21.

* **Tens** or **Jacks** or **Queens** or **Kings** &mdash; Splitting a hand with 
a value of 20 is generally a bad idea. You can stand on 20 and there's a good 
chance the dealer might have to stand on 17, 18 or 19, or go bust, in which case 
you win for having a better score. Splitting makes it likely you might have to 
stand on a low score or go bust.

## Insurance

Keep in mind that the dealer has about as much of a chance of natural blackjack 
as anyone else at the table. The general agreement seems to be that unless 
you're counting cards, insurance is a bad bet. And if you're card counting, it 
might be a good idea to lose an insurance bet here and there.
